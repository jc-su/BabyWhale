\section*{Limitations}

BabyWhale targets Apple-Silicon hardware and Python 3.14 by design
(\S\ref{sec:system}); a CUDA/CPU port is the most requested extension path
and the machine-checked-courseware mechanisms carry over unchanged. The
models are small enough to train in classroom time, so the mechanisms are
experienced directly while scale-emergent phenomena (training instabilities,
emergent capabilities, large-batch serving dynamics) are taught descriptively;
the vision module ships its configuration, tiling, and connector with a
bit-identical text path, with the pretrained encoder and multimodal training
stages staged as the next milestones. Several serving mechanisms are taught
through their governing rule (the paged-KV address translation, the batching
cohort key), and the deepest exercises---ragged attention masks, the complete
serving loop---are scoped as contributions. The repository ships no training
corpus; reproducing the reference runs uses whatever licensed data the user
supplies.

Evaluation in this paper is mechanical and reproducible: tests, drift guards,
and committed measurement scripts, including checks on the manuscript itself.
The corresponding next step is human: a controlled study of learning
outcomes, classroom deployment, and comparison against slice-specific
resources, together with exercises that modify the live model in place rather
than grading isolated mechanisms.
