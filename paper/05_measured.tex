\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We evaluate along three axes: artifact correctness
(\S\ref{sec:eval-artifact}), teaching-content accuracy
(\S\ref{sec:eval-audit}), and whether the system's claims about itself hold
when measured (\S\ref{sec:eval-measured}).

\subsection{Artifact verification}
\label{sec:eval-artifact}

The released system carries 334 tests, run in public CI on Apple-Silicon
hosts on every push: unit and integration tests for the library, one grader
per exercise (each proven to accept the reference solution \emph{and} to
reject a representative incorrect one), two milestones that train small
models to criterion inside the suite, the G2 drift and coverage guards, and a
strict documentation build. CI also performs a from-scratch environment sync
per commit---a continuous clean-machine installation test. Following G3, the
paper itself is treated as courseware: a committed script re-derives every
countable claim in this manuscript (test counts, exercise and module
inventories, the numbers in Tables~\ref{tab:ladder}--\ref{tab:biography} and
Figure~\ref{fig:loss}) from the repository and fails on any mismatch; all
checks pass at submission time.

\subsection{Content-accuracy audit}
\label{sec:eval-audit}

Before the guards existed we line-audited all 20 theory-to-code bridges
against the implementation. The audit found \textbf{8 confidently wrong
explanations}, taxonomized in Table~\ref{tab:audit}. Two properties of this
error distribution shaped the system. First, every error was
\emph{semantic}---a wrong functional form, scope, offset, factorization, or
attribution---so symbol-level checking alone cannot catch the errors that
matter; this motivates pasting real code (G2 makes the evidence visible next
to the claim) and grading exercises by behavior rather than by text (G1).
Second, the errors were written by authors with full access to the source,
suggesting that drift is a property of the writing process, not of author
diligence---hence continuous enforcement rather than one-time review.

\paragraph{The curriculum's ablation ladder, trained.}
Table~\ref{tab:ladder} trains all six architecture presets of the course's
motivation ladder under identical conditions
(\texttt{paper/experiments/run\_experiments.py}; training curves in
Appendix~\ref{app:measure}). At this scale every rung reaches statistically
indistinguishable language-model loss while its parameter and structural
costs are visible---supporting the curriculum's framing that these
mechanisms earn their keep in capabilities and serving rather than in
small-scale perplexity, and that they must therefore be \emph{measured}
there. The same script measures serving directly: Figure~\ref{fig:batching}
shows aggregate decode throughput rising from 316 to 1{,}389 tok/s as the
cohort grows from 1 to 8 requests ($4.4\times$; Appendix~\ref{app:measure}).

\paragraph{A staged lineage, evaluated.} The repository ships a full
lifecycle lineage---one $\sim$11M-parameter checkpoint chained through
pre-training, mid-training, SFT, DPO, and GRPO by the CLI---which
Table~\ref{tab:biography} evaluates stage by stage with the system's own
evaluation commands. Mid-training more than halves code-domain bits-per-byte
on text the pre-training checkpoint never saw. The lineage also exhibits two
instructive phenomena at this scale: the DPO objective destabilizes under the
shipped configuration, and GRPO drives its partial-credit reward to the
maximum while exact-solve pass@1 remains zero---a concrete instance of the
likelihood--capability gap that the course's evaluation module teaches.

\paragraph{End-to-end execution.} Figure~\ref{fig:loss} shows the course's
reference pre-training run, produced by the repository's own CLI on consumer
Apple-Silicon hardware. The scale matters pedagogically: every lifecycle
stage, including the RL loop and the batching server, completes in classroom
time on a machine learners already own, so ``break it and re-run'' is a
five-minute experiment rather than a cluster job. The analytic cost
ablations accompanying each systems module are summarized in
Figure~\ref{fig:ablations} and Appendix~\ref{app:measure}.
